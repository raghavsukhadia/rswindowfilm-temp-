A better drive begins at the glass.

Sunlight brings brightness, heat and glare. Window film helps you manage how they enter your cabin.

Automotive window film works at the glass, where sunlight first enters the vehicle. Depending on the film and shade, it can reduce incoming solar heat and UV, soften glare, and control how much visible light passes through. That balance shapes how comfortable, bright and private the cabin feels—while the view out remains an important part of choosing the right film.

Visible Light
UV
Solar Heat
  1. 01

    Manage the heat

    Reduce solar heat coming through the windows to help make time in direct sun more comfortable. Performance depends on the film selected.

  2. 02

    Filter UV

    UV-rejecting film can limit UV exposure through the glass and help care for interior surfaces exposed to sunlight.

  3. 03

    Soften the glare

    Manage harsh daylight for a more comfortable view of the road in bright conditions.

  4. 04

    Choose your view

    Visible Light Transmission, or VLT, tells you how much visible light passes through. The right balance of brightness and shade depends on the film and the glass.

  5. 05

    Shape the cabin’s appearance

    Some lower-VLT shades can give a darker look and more daytime privacy. The effect varies with lighting, glass and viewing angle.

VLT measures visible light passing through. UV rejection and IR rejection describe different parts of a film’s performance.

Heat rejection is a separate heat-control measure; it should not be replaced by an IR figure.
